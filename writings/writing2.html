Up Top

2026 · Afya Shaikh · View on Medium

The sharp sting reverberates through my hand, sending a tingling sensation down my arms, and I feel the hairs standing up. Why do people high-five so hard? Maybe it’s the rush of energy, the validation that makes me feel good. Each high-five, smile, or wave sends a warm ripple through me, causing my belly to smile and fluster. It’s a strange thing, this craving for validation from others.

I find myself constantly seeking affirmations, whether it’s the red circle around my 29 out of 30 or the simple compliment, “I love your jacket!” which brings me a smile.

But as I return home, zipping off my jacket and facing myself in the mirror, the smile fades.

In the reflection, I see a short frame, brown skin, a round face, and big brown doe eyes. Yet, despite my achievements, I struggle to recognize my own worth. Comparisons to my brothers, their seemingly effortless journey to success, weigh heavily on my mind, dragging me down like quicksand.

Alone in my thoughts, suffocating me under the weight of expectations, I cling desperately to myself, searching for comfort in the dark. The whispers of disappointment echo in my mind.

“What do you mean you have a C?”

“It’s not that hard.”

“I thought you were different.”

The words linger, suffocating me in their grip.

Salt pricks my wounded eyes.

My knees hit my chin, waterfalls down my face, and my head hangs low from the weight of it all.

But amidst the darkness, a glimmer of light shines through.

So, I hold on tight to my light, refusing to let go even as the darkness threatens to consume me.

I am my own anchor in this sea of doubt and insecurities.

I am all I need.
