Here You Go: A Mega Pack of Shopkins — Happy Birthday

2026 · Afya Shaikh · View on Medium

There is a shoebox where I painted a gradient rainbow with clouds filled with brand new shopkins.

I played in this living room with green walls, black squishy sofas, and the flat TV where I watched Littlest Pet Shop.

There was a time when that cardboard box was a world of infinite possibilities, where each toy was not just an object but a character in the grand story of my creation.

The taco was the restaurant owner, and his sweetheart, the ice cream, was part of a soap opera unfolding in my imagination. The other taco, painted in different hues, made a love triangle between the three. My Polly Pocket house was their stage, its magenta walls and orange tiled bathroom with its yellow door and unreflective purple mirror.

I constructed paper food and menus. I covered the walls with paper ads, transforming that bright house into a taco restaurant.

Now, in the living room with the black TV, brown and beige leather sofas, and the light streaming through windows on the deep brown walls, I can’t help but reflect on how much has changed.

The time when I roamed free, not caring about the world, seems like a distant memory. When all that mattered was the sandcastles and water moats on the beach and playing out in the green yard, catching frogs in the garden.

As the clock strikes midnight, I find myself buried under expectations, my white desk a battleground of unfinished assignments. The light from my laptop and iPad casts a stark glow, highlighting the reality of growing up.

The biology homework, the math equations, and the history maps mock me, reminders of the responsibilities I cannot escape.

I sit with a flutter and a flicker of my old dreams.

The dream of being older, independent, and free to explore the world. It’s a dream that now feels like a lie.

I sit here caged by my responsibilities, wondering if I’ve lost something.

The carefree days of playing with my toys seem like a distant memory. But perhaps, buried under the weight of responsibilities, there is still a glimmer of that childlike wonder. A spark that refuses to be extinguished, a reminder that even in the darkest of times, there is still room for a little bit of magic.

And so, as I gaze at that old shoebox, now a dusty gradient rainbow with clouds filled with shopkins that lie neglected and bored, I am reminded that growing up is carrying our childhood dreams with us, finding joy in the simple things, and embracing the innocence that once defined us.

So, I pick up the box high up on the lone shelf.

And I dream about the future. Once again.
