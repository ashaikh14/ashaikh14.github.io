Life Until Eleven

2025 · Afya Shaikh · View on Medium

Late-night evenings start as a conversation over dinner.

Turn to sitting on the striped rug in the kitchen against the cream-colored cabinets under the square chandelier.

Laughing loudly at each of our life stories.

Hearing mom yell from the stairs railing to quiet down.

We giggle, listening to his anecdotes from his college life.

He smiles, lines creasing his dark eyes, messy straight black hair, and a beard making him look more mature than he is.

Me and my brother, black curly hair, tan skin, and a growing beard, cackle hearing about how my oldest brother got into a knife fight his first year of college and now has a scar over his heart as a memory.

But in reality, he just got hit by opening a car door while he was on a hill.

Our laughter settled, and I asked if he felt embarrassed about the scar that would mark his heart forever. He shrugged it off effortlessly, telling me it was funny.

I couldn’t help but question him about his reaction, wondering how he managed to brush it off with such ease.

For me, clinging to my mistakes and disappointments was a tough habit to break. I had a tendency to chain them up, burying them deep and pretending they didn’t exist, even if they were elephants in my mind.

“Look,” my brother said, his big brown almond-shaped eyes staring into mine, “you might hold on to your mistakes, but they should shape you, not define you. Don’t lock them away. Learn from them, or else they will cage you forever.”

His words hit home.

I closed my eyes, took a deep breath, and exhaled, letting go of the chains and locks that had held me captive. I could feel their weight crumbling to the ends of my mind, and a sense of freedom washed over me.

I looked at my brothers, our eyes connecting. They smiled, knowing they helped me navigate the boulder we all had carried once.

And without a word, we laughed, a mix of silliness and seriousness.

As we talk about life until eleven.
