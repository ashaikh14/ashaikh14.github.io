Hide the Diamond, but Shine like the Sun

2025 · Afya Shaikh · View on Medium

As I walked into the first day of kindergarten, my heart raced, and I felt like the hammering of construction in New York. I could feel the eyes of everyone on me. It was as if the hijab that I had chosen to wear was a neon sign, flashing Different! to the whole world. At first, it was overwhelming. I felt like I was in the spotlight with the light shining in my eyes on a stage with eyes tracking my every move, and I hated it.

The stares, the whispers, the feeling of being constantly judged. I wanted to hide away, to take off the hijab and pretend that I wasn’t different.

So, I did.

I took it off and laughed at it, pretending it was all a joke. I thought that if I could blend in, I could finally be like everyone else,

Then, I would finally feel

accepted.

I was only six.

It was like the hijab was a boulder on my head.

A boulder made of shame, guilt, and difference.

I hid who I was by taking it off every day at school and wearing it on the bus.

I didn’t understand the meaning of it.

I just wanted to make my parents proud.

I wanted to wear pigtails and have everyone see it like the other girls.

I wanted to hide my hair and have no one see it, like the girls in my family.

I just wanted to fit in.

To squeeze into the small gap of societal expectations.

Shaping and molding and sucking in, crafting and remaking myself into someone I wasn’t.

I didn’t understand why I was so weird for wearing something different.

Shouldn’t that make me unique?

Instead, I was judged.

Laughed at.

Torn through.

And no, I didn’t stand up for myself.

I cried with a wobbly mouth, tears, and all.

I asked my parents, the salty tears painting my face. Wondering why I was so

Different.

Then, my dad told me something I would never forget.

He said,

“Afya, in Islam, women are often compared to diamonds — precious, valuable, and beautiful. But do people display their valuables to the world to risk theft or damage? No, they hide it away, treasuring and protecting it. But, even when hidden, the beauty of the diamond still shines.”

I smiled, my cheeks rounding up as I rubbed my arm over my weak eyes.

It reminded me of the sun.

How, even if the clouds covered the sun, the warmth and beauty of it still shine through.

As the days progressed, I saw the hijab as a symbol of my faith, modesty, and culture. And whenever the difference catches up to me, I look up to the sun.

And I feel its warm embrace and happy smile, even behind the clouds.
